## INTRO: HOW LONG IS THE BREAK?

*Federal House of Commons vs. Ontario Legislative Assembly — recent summers*

| Year | Federal (HoC) | Ontario (LA) | Ontario–Federal gap |
|------|--------------|--------------|-------------------|
| 2019 | ~12 wks (SO-anchored) | ~20 wks | ~8 wks |
| 2021 | ~12 wks | ~14.5 wks (COVID; shortened) | ~2.5 wks |
| 2024 | ~12 wks (Jun. 20 → Sept. 16) | ~19–21 wks (~Jun. 6 → Oct. 21) | ~7–9 wks |
| 2025 | ~12 wks (SO-anchored) | ~19 wks (~Jun. 9 → ~Oct. 16) | ~7 wks |
| 2026 | ~12 wks (SO-anchored) | ~21 wks (~Jun. 3 → ~Oct. 26) | ~9 wks |

**Why the federal figure is consistent:** The federal summer break is set by Standing Order 28(2). The SO anchors the summer adjournment around June 23 and the calendar — tabled by the Speaker by September 30 each year — produces approximately 12 weeks off in summer across all years the House sits continuously. The figure is not a rule but a structural output of the calendar.

**Why the Ontario figure varies:** Ontario has no equivalent Standing Order. The length of the summer break is set by government motion each session. The motion is tabled and passed by the government of the day; the opposition may object but cannot block it. There is no floor, no ceiling, no procedural constraint on length in the Standing Orders. ## MAIN: WHAT STOPS WHEN THE LEGISLATURE RISES

The summer break is an adjournment, not a prorogation. The distinction matters.

At **prorogation**, the session ends: bills die, committees dissolve, the order paper is wiped. At **adjournment**, the session continues — the House is simply not sitting. Bills remain on the order paper. But the mechanisms of day-to-day accountability stop.

Here is what suspends:

---

### 1. Question Period

**Status: suspended for the duration.**

Oral questions to ministers exist only when the House is sitting. There is no mechanism — in the federal Standing Orders or Ontario's — for oral questions during adjournment. Ministers are not required to appear, respond, or account publicly to elected members during a summer recess.

Federal: QP is governed by Standing Order 30. [VERBATIM NEEDED: SO 30 text from ourcommons.ca/procedure/standing-orders/Chap3-e.html]
Ontario: QP governed by Standing Order 37. [VERBATIM NEEDED: Ontario SO 37 from January 2025 PDF]

---

### 2. Committee hearings

**Status: suspended unless specifically authorized by government motion.**

Committees do not sit as of right during adjournment. A government motion is required to authorize specific committees to meet during the break. The authorization is selective: only named committees are permitted, and only for named purposes.

**Federal:** Committees can be authorized to sit during adjournment under SO 106(4) or by special order. In practice, authorization is selective and requires a House order before the rise date. [VERBATIM NEEDED: SO 106 text]

**Ontario example on record:** The summer 2024 adjournment motion (Parliament 43, Session 1) specifically authorized two committees to meet during the break: the Standing Committee on Procedure and House Affairs, and the Standing Committee on Heritage, Infrastructure and Cultural Policy. Other standing committees were not authorized. A separate order addressed the Standing Committee on Justice Policy (Intimate Partner Violence study), reinstating it under specified conditions. All other committee work halted.

### 3. Written questions

**Status: suspended — questions accumulate on the order paper, unanswered.**

Written questions (also called questions on notice or order-paper questions) are submitted in writing by members and require written responses from ministers within a set period. During adjournment, the clock effectively stops. Questions submitted before the rise date receive no response until the House returns.

**Federal:** Written questions are governed by Standing Order 39. Ministers have 45 days to respond. During a 12-week federal summer recess, any question submitted in the weeks before the rise date will wait the full duration before the response period resumes. ### 4. Bills on the order paper

**Status: survive — this is the key distinction from prorogation.**

Unlike prorogation, adjournment does not wipe the order paper. Government bills, private members' bills, and all other business in progress remain at their current stage when the House returns. No reintroduction is required.

**Federal:** At prorogation, bills die; at adjournment, they do not. The procedural basis is that adjournment is an interruption within a session, not the end of one. *Procedure and Practice*, 4th ed. (2025), Chapter 8 distinguishes adjournment from prorogation on exactly this basis. [SOURCE: ourcommons.ca/procedure/procedure-and-practice-4/ch08-7-e.html — URL confirmed, human-fetchable]

**Ontario:** Same structure applies. At prorogation, all unfinished business is "entirely terminated" and bills must be reintroduced as if they had never existed (confirmed from procedural summaries; the rule has been documented in coverage of Ontario prorogations). During adjournment, the order paper is preserved.

---

### 5. Recall during adjournment

**Status: possible, but at the government's effective initiative (federal) or by convention (Ontario).**

**Federal:** SO 28(3) allows the Speaker to recall the House during adjournment if satisfied that the public interest requires it, on the written request of at least 20 members. In practice, the government controls whether this is invoked. [VERBATIM NEEDED: SO 28(3) full text]

**Ontario:** The Lieutenant Governor may summon the Legislature at any time. There is no Standing Order equivalent to the federal SO 28(3) recall-by-members mechanism. Recall is executive prerogative, not a member-initiated procedural right. [VERBATIM NEEDED: Confirm absence of recall mechanism in Ontario SOs — January 2025 PDF]

## STRUCTURAL FINDING

The summer recess is not procedurally neutral. During a break:

- The executive is not publicly questioned by elected members.
- Committee oversight is suspended except where the executive specifically chooses to authorize it.
- Written-question response obligations are deferred.

Bills survive intact — so the recess does not reset legislative work. But the scrutiny mechanisms that operate on a daily sitting schedule are off for the duration.

The federal break runs approximately 12 weeks, fixed in structure by SO 28(2). The Ontario break has run between 14 and 21 weeks in recent sessions, with no procedural constraint on its length — set instead by government motion.

The structural difference: one is a rule. One is a decision.
