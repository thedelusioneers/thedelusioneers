## FRAME 1 — Fixed vs discretionary

### Federal: Standing Order 28(2)

**Sourceability: SOURCEABLE**

Primary instrument: *Standing Orders of the House of Commons (as of April 29, 2025)*, Appendix 17, Procedure and Practice 4th Edition (2025).
URL: `https://www.ourcommons.ca/procedure/procedure-and-practice-4/app17-e.html`

Also available as standalone PDF:
`https://www.ourcommons.ca/procedure/standing-orders/SOPDF.pdf`
Status: Confirmed, fetch blocked — same status.

The full standing-orders chapter breakdown is at:
`https://www.ourcommons.ca/procedure/standing-orders/Chap3-e.html` (Chapter 3 covers sittings)

**What the sources confirm about SO 28(2):**

Confirmed via *Procedure and Practice*, 4th ed., Chapter 8, and multiple procedural summaries:

- SO 28(2) establishes the parliamentary calendar structure. The Speaker shall, by September 30 each year, after consultation with House Leaders, table a calendar for the following year setting out sitting and non-sitting weeks between the last Monday in January and the Monday following Easter Monday.
- The calendar provides for **approximately 135 sitting days** when the House sits continuously for a calendar year. This figure is a **structural result** of the calendar, **not a fixed rule or minimum floor**. There is no Standing Order prescribing a minimum number of sitting days.
- The summer adjournment falls around **June 23** — confirmed by the extended-hours provision (SO 27(1)), which allows a minister to move extended hours on the 10th sitting day preceding June 23.
- Three major adjournment periods: Christmas (~7 weeks), Easter (~2 weeks), and summer (~12 weeks). Four additional brief adjournments during the year (~1 week each).
- Historical note: Calendar first adopted November 1982 (Lefebvre Committee recommendations). Extended slightly in 1991 and adjusted in 2001.

**Claim status:**
- "SO 28(2) fixes the federal summer adjournment": **CONFIRMED** — it sets the structure and anchor date (around June 23) in the Standing Orders.
- "135 sitting days is a rule": **REFUTED** — it is a structural approximation, not a codified floor.
- "The calendar is tabled, not voted": **CONFIRMED** — Speaker tables the calendar after consultation; it is not subject to a House vote.

**Tabled sitting calendar:**
URL: `https://www.ourcommons.ca/en/sitting-calendar`
Status: URL confirmed. Current and historical calendars available here. Direct fetch blocked.

### Ontario: Standing Orders — existence of any fixed floor

**Sourceability: SOURCEABLE (absence is the finding)**

Primary instrument: *Standing Orders of the Legislative Assembly of Ontario*, January 2025.
URL (PDF): `https://www.ola.org/sites/default/files/common/pdf/standing-orders-january-2025-en.pdf`
Status: URL confirmed, direct machine fetch blocked (HTTP 403). Human-fetchable.

Also available at: `https://www.ola.org/en/legislative-business/standing-orders`

**What the sources confirm:**

- No Standing Order provides a fixed sitting-day floor, minimum annual sitting requirement, or maximum recess-length limit. Multiple searches and procedural summaries confirm the absence of such a provision.
- Ontario Standing Orders govern daily sitting times (Mon–Thu), the mechanism for adjournment within a day, and recess within a meeting — but do not set an annual calendar structure equivalent to federal SO 28(2).
- **The Ontario calendar for a given year is set by government motion** — not by Standing Order. The 2025 calendar (confirmed from ola.org search results) reads: "The House may meet from Monday to Thursday from April 14, 2025, to December 11, 2025" with exceptions (summer break) from approximately June 9 to October 16, 2025 — a gap of ~19 weeks.
- The 2026 calendar shows exceptions from approximately June 3 to October 26, 2026 (~21 weeks).

**Structural finding (confirmed):** The Ontario mechanism is discretionary. There is no Standing Order equivalent to federal SO 28(2). The summer recess length is determined by the government of the day through a motion, not by rule.

## FRAME 3 — Accountability cost at adjournment

### Federal: What survives adjournment

**Sourceability: SOURCEABLE**

Primary instrument: *Procedure and Practice*, 4th ed. (2025), Chapter 8 (Prorogation and Dissolution) and Chapter 9 (Daily Sitting); Standing Orders, esp. SO 28(4) and SO 86 et seq.
URLs:
- `https://www.ourcommons.ca/procedure/procedure-and-practice-4/ch08-7-e.html` (Prorogation and Dissolution)
- `https://www.ourcommons.ca/procedure/procedure-and-practice-4/ch08-4-e.html` (House Calendar)
Status: URLs confirmed; direct machine fetch blocked. Human-accessible.

**What the sources confirm:**

During **adjournment** (summer recess), as distinct from prorogation:
- Bills on the order paper **survive** — the order paper is preserved. The House simply isn't sitting.
- Committee mandates technically continue, but committees cannot meet unless specifically authorized by a government motion to sit during the adjournment.
- Question Period is suspended — no mechanism for oral questions during recess.
- Written questions on the order paper accumulate but receive no answers until the House returns.
- The government can be recalled during an adjournment via SO 28(3) if the Speaker is satisfied circumstances warrant it.

During **prorogation**:
- All unfinished bills die on the order paper and must be reintroduced.
- Committee memberships lapse; orders of reference expire.
- Exception since 2003: Private Members' Business (bills and motions originating in the House) carries over session to session.
- Unanswered written questions die.
- Exception: Orders and addresses for returns/papers carry over within the same Parliament.
- Senate bills: SO provides for reinstatement within 60 days of a new session.

**Key procedural distinction:** Summer recess is an adjournment, not a prorogation. Bills survive. But question period, committee hearings, and written-question answers are all suspended for the duration.

**Claim status:**
- "Bills die during summer recess": **REFUTED** — they die at prorogation, not adjournment.
- "Committees stop during summer recess": **CONFIRMED** — unless specifically authorized by motion.
- "Question period is suspended during recess": **CONFIRMED** — no mechanism exists for QP during adjournment.

### Ontario: What survives adjournment

**Sourceability: PARTIAL**

Primary instrument: *Standing Orders of the Legislative Assembly of Ontario*, January 2025.
URL: `https://www.ola.org/sites/default/files/common/pdf/standing-orders-january-2025-en.pdf`
Status: Direct fetch blocked.

**What the sources confirm:**

During **prorogation** in Ontario:
- All unfinished bills die and must be reintroduced (confirmed from multiple sources, including CBC coverage of 104 Ontario bills dying at 2012 prorogation).
- Committees lose power to transact business.
- Exception: Orders and addresses for returns/papers carry over (Standing Order confirmed in procedural summaries).

During **adjournment** in Ontario:
- Parallel to federal: bills on the order paper survive; the order paper is preserved.
- Committees generally cannot meet unless authorized. The 2024 summer adjournment motion specifically authorized certain standing committees (Procedure and House Affairs; Heritage, Infrastructure and Cultural Policy) to meet at the call of the Chair — confirming the default is that committees cannot meet without this authorization.
- Question Period is suspended.
- The Standing Orders permit written questions; their fate during extended summer recess is not confirmed from available sources (gap — need direct SO text).

**Gap:** The specific Ontario Standing Order text governing what happens to written questions and private members' bills during adjournment (as distinct from prorogation) requires direct retrieval of the January 2025 SO PDF.
