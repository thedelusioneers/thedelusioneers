The Receipt — Bell Edition

Date: 2026-06-13
Jurisdiction: Federal (CRTC/CCTS) + Ontario
Format: Receipt

The factual claims in this piece are classified PARTIAL. BCE investor relations URLs (bce.ca, SEC EDGAR, SEDAR+), the CRTC Internet Code text (crtc.gc.ca, canlii.org, laws-lois.justice.gc.ca), and the CCTS complaint-process page (ccts-cprst.ca) were inaccessible from the production environment on 2026-06-13 due to a total external-HTTP block. All figures and regulatory provisions were verified against primary sources in April 2026. The absence of written confirmation for the retention rate and the absence of a published per-complaint cost to Bell are confirmed gaps in the record, not retrieval failures.

Line Item 1 The Charge

Bell Canada applied a $6/month increase to residential internet accounts in Ontario.

A Bell customer in Kingston received this increase on their monthly billing statement. The notice of the increase appeared on page 3 of that statement.

Line Item 2 The Math
Monthly increase per subscriber $6.00
Bell retail high-speed internet subscribers (Canada, Q4 2025) 4,455,460
Projected additional monthly revenue ~$26,732,760
Projected additional annual revenue ~$320,793,120

Bell reported 4,455,460 retail high-speed internet subscribers in Canada at the end of Q4 2025. At $6 per subscriber per month, the increase projects to approximately $26.7 million in additional monthly revenue and approximately $320.8 million in additional annual revenue.

Line Item 3 The Rule

The CRTC Internet Code (Telecom Regulatory Policy 2019-269, in force) requires that a provider give advance written notice before implementing a price change of this kind.

The Internet Code specifies that notice must be in writing. The Internet Code does not specify where within a billing statement that notice must appear.

Bell gave written notice of the increase. The notice appeared on page 3 of the billing statement.

The distinction the record establishes: notice was given. Whether notice was findable is a question the Internet Code does not resolve.

Line Item 4 The Retention Call

A Bell customer in Kingston contacted Bell following receipt of the $6 increase. A Bell retention representative described a rate reduction as available and described it as permanent.

Gap‑01

The record does not disclose a written confirmation of the permanent rate. No written confirmation was provided by Bell at the time of the retention call.

The distinction the record establishes: the verbal statement and any written confirmation are separate instruments. The written confirmation is absent from the record.

Line Item 5 The Remedy

A consumer may file a complaint with the Commission for Complaints for Telecom-television Services (CCTS). Filing is free to the consumer. The provider must respond within a fixed window. Compensation up to a stated maximum is available.

Gap‑02

The record does not disclose the exact administrative cost to Bell of processing a CCTS complaint. That figure is not published at a confirmable per-complaint dollar amount.

The directional finding in the record: the administrative cost to Bell of processing a CCTS complaint may exceed the $6 monthly increase. The record does not confirm this to a dollar amount.
